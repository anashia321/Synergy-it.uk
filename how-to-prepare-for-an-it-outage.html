Insight

How to prepare for an IT outage.

An IT outage can interrupt communication, access to systems and everyday work. A practical continuity plan helps UK businesses respond calmly, protect important information and restore essential services in the right order.

Let’s talk ↗
The practical approach

Resilience starts before something goes wrong.

Outage planning is not about predicting every possible incident. It is about identifying the systems your business cannot operate without, deciding who owns the response and documenting the first actions people should take.

That plan should reflect the way your business actually works. Review your critical applications, connectivity, devices, identity systems, backups and supplier dependencies. Then test the recovery process so gaps are found during a planned exercise rather than during a live disruption.

01

Identify critical services

Prioritise the systems, data and communication channels your team needs to keep operating.

02

Set clear responsibilities

Give named people clear roles for escalation, communication, supplier contact and recovery decisions.

03

Test and improve

Run simple recovery exercises and update the plan whenever your systems, suppliers or team structure changes.

FAQ

Common questions about outage preparation.

How often should an IT continuity plan be reviewed?

Review it at least annually and whenever you introduce important systems, change suppliers, move office or grow the team significantly.

Are backups enough to recover from an outage?

Backups are important, but recovery also depends on access, documented procedures, tested restoration and clear ownership.

Can a small business benefit from outage planning?

Yes. A short, understandable plan can make a meaningful difference to recovery time and decision-making, even for a small team.
